### THE RECURRING EXPLANATORY CYCLE

Begin with a situation students can understand. Ask them to predict the outcome before revealing it. Step through the mechanism at the instructor’s pace. Change one clearly labeled condition. Compare outcomes under the same relevant assumptions. End with a principle that explains the difference.

An Anchor scene sustains this cycle long enough for a real prediction and comparison. A Bridge scene is a shorter explanation or connection, often a static matrix or a few purposeful reveals. These are teaching roles, not required visual components or fixed durations.

Do not turn the detailed instructor prose into slide paragraphs. The screen should contain the situation, visual evidence, a concise question, and the principle when revealed. Students should be able to understand the printed version through selected states and short explanatory captions.

# WEEK 01 | ENGINEERING SOFTWARE THAT PEOPLE CAN DEPEND ON

Research and curriculum date: 16 September 2026

What makes working code an engineered system?

Instructor lecture plan | flexible pacing | 8 scenes: 3 Anchors + 5 Bridges | planned HTML: weeks/week-01.html

## 1. PURPOSE, CASE STATE, AND SCOPE

Open with a familiar problem rather than a history of programming languages. Campus Rooms appears to reserve a room successfully, yet two people receive confirmations for the same time. The lecture gradually widens the frame from a function that runs to a service whose behavior, users, changes, and consequences must be understood.

Entry knowledge is the ability to read a small function, an if-statement, and a collection. Do not require a particular programming language or familiarity with enterprise tooling. Establish vocabulary through the example: stakeholder, requirement, constraint, quality attribute, evidence, lifecycle, and trade-off. The weekly ordering is a learning sequence, not a claim that real engineering happens in fourteen isolated phases. [S01, S02, S24]

State the through-line in the opening and return to it: every habit this course builds, a precise rule, an independent check, a bounded permission, a reviewable change, is also what it takes to direct and check automated engineering assistance. Weeks 2, 7, 8, 9, 10 and 12 each carry one short beat on that claim inside an existing scene, and Week 13 examines it fully. Decisions in force for the whole package are listed in 00_START_HERE.txt.

In practice (dated examples, 2026): teams keep requirements and incident reports in issue trackers such as GitHub Issues or Jira, check accessibility with axe or Lighthouse next to a keyboard-only walk-through, and draw context maps in Mermaid or draw.io.

## 2. OBSERVABLE LEARNING OUTCOMES

2. Identify stakeholders, system boundaries, and competing quality concerns.

3. Explain why delivery and maintenance belong in the engineering lifecycle.

4. Separate an observation, an assumption, and evidence supporting a claim.

5. Explain one technical decision in terms of benefit, cost, and affected people.

## 5. DETAILED SCENE TEACHING NOTES

### 01. TWO GREEN CONFIRMATIONS CANNOT BOTH BE RIGHT

Anchor scene | ID: two-confirmations

**VISUAL IDEA:** Two browser cards beside one shared room calendar. Three controlled beats reveal request, stored state, and user consequence.

**SITUATION, PREDICTION, AND MECHANISM:** Situation and prediction: Ari and Bo each see “Confirmed” for C101 at 10:00. Ask, “What evidence would let us decide whether the system worked?” Reveal the calendar containing two overlapping confirmed bookings, then the people arriving at the same door. Show that an attractive interface and a successful response are not the whole behavior.

**CHANGE ONE CONDITION AND COMPARE:** Change only the confirmation rule: a shared decision now admits one request and rejects the conflicting one. Compare inconvenience from an honest rejection with harm from a false promise. Do not explain transactions yet; preserve that question for later.

**PRINCIPLE TO REVEAL:** Engineering judges behavior against an intended outcome and its consequences, not appearance alone.

**STUDENT PDF TREATMENT:** Print the two-confirmation outcome and the one-confirmation outcome, with the violated rule written below both.

### 02. THE BOOKING BUTTON IS NOT THE WHOLE SYSTEM

Bridge scene | ID: system-boundary

**VISUAL IDEA:** A context map with the service inside a boundary and users, campus identity, room records, and notification delivery outside.

**SITUATION, PREDICTION, AND MECHANISM:** Ask which parts the team can change directly. Reveal one interaction at a time and label arrows with their meaning. A student request, an identity assertion, and a notification are not interchangeable kinds of information.

**CHANGE ONE CONDITION AND COMPARE:** Make the notification provider unavailable without changing room storage. Ask which user outcomes remain possible. Keep the boundary fixed so the class sees a dependency rather than a new diagram.

**PRINCIPLE TO REVEAL:** A system boundary identifies responsibility and dependencies; it does not make external behavior disappear.

**STUDENT PDF TREATMENT:** Use one labeled context map with the unavailable dependency marked by both text and a broken-line symbol.

### 03. THE SAME SCREEN CAN SERVE ONE PERSON AND EXCLUDE ANOTHER

Anchor scene | ID: stakeholder-lenses

**VISUAL IDEA:** A stakeholder map connected to concrete concerns; an accessible keyboard path sits beside the visual interface.

**SITUATION, PREDICTION, AND MECHANISM:** Ask who is affected besides the person clicking Reserve. Reveal a student needing step-free access, room staff handling closures, support staff diagnosing mistakes, and a maintainer interpreting old decisions. Translate each concern into a question the system must answer.

**CHANGE ONE CONDITION AND COMPARE:** Change the user interaction from mouse to keyboard. The visually polished version initially has no reachable confirmation control; the corrected version does. Compare task completion, not the attractiveness of the two screens. Introduce accessibility as part of whether the service works. [S25]

**PRINCIPLE TO REVEAL:** Quality depends on the people and conditions included in the definition of success.

**STUDENT PDF TREATMENT:** Print the stakeholder-concern table and the successful keyboard route; describe the failed route in text.

### 04. FASTER IS NOT A COMPLETE DESCRIPTION OF BETTER

Bridge scene | ID: quality-tradeoffs

**VISUAL IDEA:** A comparison table with response time, correctness, recoverability, privacy, and change effort; avoid an unexplained radar chart.

**SITUATION, PREDICTION, AND MECHANISM:** Offer two fictional designs: immediate confirmation from an unchecked local view, and confirmation after an authoritative availability decision. Ask which is better before revealing their behavior. Separate quality attributes from optional features. Name the standard vocabulary once: ISO/IEC 25010:2023 lists nine product quality characteristics, among them performance efficiency, reliability, security, interaction capability and maintainability. [S46]

**CHANGE ONE CONDITION AND COMPARE:** Keep correctness mandatory and change the response-time target. Discuss which optimizations preserve the rule and which merely hide a delay. Clearly label illustrative measurements and never imply that accuracy must always be traded for speed.

**PRINCIPLE TO REVEAL:** Compare alternatives under stated constraints; some requirements are conditions of acceptance rather than points in a score.

**STUDENT PDF TREATMENT:** Print the alternatives, assumptions, and a short rationale rather than a winner badge.

### 05. A FAILURE CAN TEACH THE NEXT REQUIREMENT

Anchor scene | ID: feedback-lifecycle

**VISUAL IDEA:** A loop connecting need, model, implementation, checks, release, operation, and revision; an incident card travels through it.

**SITUATION, PREDICTION, AND MECHANISM:** Predict where a missing “notification failed” behavior would be discovered. Reveal that it may emerge during a requirements conversation, an integration check, or operation. Follow the incident back to an updated user message and a new verification check.

**CHANGE ONE CONDITION AND COMPARE:** Move the first useful feedback earlier while keeping the defect the same. Compare the kinds of rework involved, without presenting an invented universal cost-of-defect multiplier. Explain iteration as revisiting understanding and increment as adding usable capability.

**PRINCIPLE TO REVEAL:** The lifecycle is a network of feedback loops, not a one-way conveyor ending when code runs.

**STUDENT PDF TREATMENT:** Print the full loop and two annotated feedback paths, including what information changed.

### 06. WHAT WOULD MAKE US BELIEVE THIS WORKS?

Bridge scene | ID: evidence-not-confidence

**VISUAL IDEA:** Three columns labeled Claim, Check, and Remaining uncertainty; cards move only when a reason is supplied.

**SITUATION, PREDICTION, AND MECHANISM:** Present “It looks right,” “It passed one example,” and “It met the stated checks under these conditions.” Ask which evidence supports which claim. Reveal a counterexample outside the tested conditions without implying that all prior testing was worthless.

**CHANGE ONE CONDITION AND COMPARE:** Change the workload from one user to simultaneous users. Compare the unchanged claim with the newly exposed assumption. Introduce the course habit of stating both evidence and its limits.

**PRINCIPLE TO REVEAL:** A check supports a bounded claim; confidence without scope is difficult to evaluate.

**STUDENT PDF TREATMENT:** Print the completed claim-check-uncertainty table, retaining the counterexample.

### 07. WHO CARRIES THE COST OF AN ENGINEERING SHORTCUT?

Bridge scene | ID: responsibility-and-impact

**VISUAL IDEA:** A consequence map linking a decision to users, support effort, data exposure, and future change.

**SITUATION, PREDICTION, AND MECHANISM:** Show a support log containing more personal information than needed to diagnose a failure. Ask who benefits and who takes the risk. Reveal an alternative containing a request identifier and failure category instead of private booking details.

**CHANGE ONE CONDITION AND COMPARE:** Change the support question so that one additional field is genuinely needed. Compare deliberate, justified collection with collecting everything by default. Keep this an engineering discussion, not jurisdiction-specific legal advice.

**PRINCIPLE TO REVEAL:** Professional responsibility includes the consequences imposed on people who did not make the technical choice.

**STUDENT PDF TREATMENT:** Print the minimal diagnostic record and the rationale for each retained field.

### 08. ONE DOMAIN, MANY KINDS OF EXPLANATION

Bridge scene | ID: course-evidence-map

**VISUAL IDEA:** A course map that changes representation: requirement card, state diagram, dependency graph, test table, release path, and incident timeline.

**SITUATION, PREDICTION, AND MECHANISM:** Ask whether one flowchart could explain all of these questions equally well. Reveal each representation with a single example of the question it answers. Introduce the recurring Campus Rooms case without exposing authoring tools.

**CHANGE ONE CONDITION AND COMPARE:** Keep the booking behavior fixed and switch from “Who depends on this?” to “What happened first?” Compare the dependency graph with the sequence diagram. Then change the actor: the same booking change is drafted by an automated assistant instead of a colleague. Ask which representations and checks are no longer needed. None; that is the through-line the course returns to in Week 13.

**PRINCIPLE TO REVEAL:** Choose a representation for the question, not a diagram style for the whole course.

**STUDENT PDF TREATMENT:** Print the course map as a question-to-representation reference, including the row for a change drafted by an automated assistant.

## 6. TEACHING GUARDRAILS

Define software engineering broadly through change, coordination, quality, and the system's lifetime rather than a contest between programmers and engineers. The course does not teach that every small script needs heavyweight process. Keep process proportional to consequence and uncertainty. Treat security, accessibility, and maintainability as recurring concerns from the first week. [S01, S02, S24]

In every visual, distinguish a hypothetical failure from an observed real incident. Do not use fabricated industry percentages to make the opening dramatic. The booking conflict is a counterexample that motivates inquiry, not an explanation of its implementation yet.

## 7. MISCONCEPTION AND DIAGNOSIS CLINIC

“The interface says success, so the operation succeeded.” Ask which authoritative state supports that message.

“More process always means better engineering.” Compare a disposable local calculation with a shared booking service and ask what risks differ.

“Testing happens only at the end.” Revisit the feedback loop and identify an early check on a requirement.

## 8. IN-LECTURE CHECKS WITH ANSWERS

QUESTION 1: What is wrong with two successful overlapping confirmations?

**ANSWER:** They violate the intended exclusivity rule even when each screen and request appears successful.

QUESTION 2: Why include support staff in the stakeholder map?

**ANSWER:** Their diagnostic needs influence observability, documentation, privacy, and recovery decisions.

QUESTION 3: Does passing one booking example establish correctness for simultaneous requests?

**ANSWER:** No. The evidence does not cover that interaction condition.

QUESTION 4: Which representation answers “What happens after a timeout?”

**ANSWER:** A state model or a sequence/timeline view, depending on whether the question concerns possible states or a particular interaction.

### 02. AN OMITTED USER CAN HIDE AN ENTIRE REQUIREMENT

Bridge scene | ID: elicitation-and-scope

**VISUAL IDEA:** A context map gains questions at its boundaries; facts, assumptions, and open decisions have different labels and shapes.

**SITUATION, PREDICTION, AND MECHANISM:** Ask what must be learned before accepting a request from a guest. Reveal questions about identity, cancellation rights, accessibility, and room closures. Compare the requested solution with the underlying need to know the outcome. A small interface prototype distinguishes Submitting, Confirmed and Unknown outcome; its walkthrough informs the requirement, while the authoritative stored result remains a separate engineering concern. Label this as a simulated observation, not user research that the course actually conducted.

**CHANGE ONE CONDITION AND COMPARE:** Introduce an external visitor as a new stakeholder but keep this release's campus-member boundary. Compare explicitly deferred behavior with an accidental omission.

**PRINCIPLE TO REVEAL:** An explicit boundary and an open-question register make uncertainty discussable.

**STUDENT PDF TREATMENT:** Print the scope boundary and a compact fact-assumption-question table. Retain the requested email, observed uncertainty and prototype comparison so the elicitation method is visible.

## 7. MISCONCEPTION AND DIAGNOSIS CLINIC

“A mock proves the external service works.” It observes behavior under the assumptions encoded in the substitute.

“Complete coverage means correct software.” Execution evidence does not establish that assertions or requirements are sufficient.

## 8. IN-LECTURE CHECKS WITH ANSWERS

QUESTION 1: Which two cases distinguish an inclusive 120-minute limit from no upper limit?

**ANSWER:** 120 must be accepted and 121 rejected, under otherwise valid conditions.

QUESTION 2: Do adjacent intervals overlap under the course rule?

**ANSWER:** No. The end of one may equal the start of the next because the intervals are half-open.

QUESTION 3: Why test the storage boundary as well as the overlap function?

**ANSWER:** Correct local arithmetic alone does not protect the invariant against concurrent operations.

QUESTION 4: Why can implementation and test agree while both are wrong?

**ANSWER:** They may share the same mistaken assumption or calculate the expected result using the defective logic.

QUESTION 5: Does a controlled test double establish that the real provider honors its contract?

**ANSWER:** No. It isolates a claim; evidence at the real boundary is still needed.

QUESTION 6: A file converter passes a test whose expected output is produced by the same conversion function. What would strengthen the test?

**ANSWER:** Use an independently derived small example and a property justified by the format's meaning, including a relevant boundary. If round-trip conversion is promised, check it too; a shared defect can make both directions agree while still producing the wrong result.

## 8. IN-LECTURE CHECKS WITH ANSWERS

QUESTION 1: Why retain the existing affected booking rather than silently deleting it?

**ANSWER:** Its owner already relied on the promise; explicit affected status, history, and an authorized resolution preserve information needed to address the consequence.

QUESTION 2: Where must closure and booking ordering be enforced?

**ANSWER:** At the authoritative decision boundary with a mechanism that preserves the stated concurrent behavior.

QUESTION 3: What is missing when function checks pass but users cannot perceive an affected booking?

**ANSWER:** Evidence that the complete user-facing requirement, including accessible status information, has been satisfied.

QUESTION 4: What should happen to the final engineering claim when an assumption changes?

**ANSWER:** Reconsider the affected decision and narrow or revise the claim until appropriate evidence is available.

QUESTION 5: Does a sponsor's approval resolve the known absence of accessible affected-booking information?

**ANSWER:** No. The user consequence remains. Explain who is affected, offer a safer alternative and escalate the unresolved decision rather than treating approval as a repair.

QUESTION 6: For a device-configuration update, which parts of this course's release reasoning transfer, and what must be learned again?

**ANSWER:** Trace the need to behavior, model failure, verify the candidate, control authority and plan recovery. Elicit the device's interruption behavior, safe operating state, update constraints and consequences of failure; room-booking policies and web-service rollout assumptions do not transfer automatically.

### A STABLE CONTROL CONTRACT

Previous slide and Next slide always change the lecture page. They never mean “reveal the next animation beat.” Next slide is still allowed when a scene is unfinished, so an instructor can deliberately skip ahead.

Step advances one meaningful state within the current scene. Back step returns to the previous scene state. At the final state, Step becomes disabled or clearly finished; it never silently advances to another slide. A continuous Run/Pause control is optional only for scenes such as queue flow, and the initial state is paused.

Replay returns to the first step under the currently selected condition or comparison preset. Reset restores the canonical baseline, all default conditions, and the first paused step. These actions must not be visually or semantically interchangeable.

Default re-entry behavior is deterministic: returning to a scene restores its canonical baseline, paused. A direct hash link does the same. Any optional resume behavior must be a clearly chosen mode, not hidden persistence. Leaving a scene cancels its timers and animation work so nothing continues changing offscreen.

Changing a condition returns that scenario to its first paused state. Keep the comparison baseline visible or make it easy to restore. Display the selected condition in text so the viewer knows exactly what changed.

### FREEDOM DURING AUTHORING

The plan fixes learning intent, case semantics, core comparisons, control meanings, and evidence. The authoring agent can improve composition, transitions, visual metaphors, typography, component organization, and small supporting examples. It may split a crowded scene or simplify an animation when that improves comprehension.

Do not let visual invention change a rule, invent a measurement, hide a failure, introduce a runtime external dependency, or replace a comparison with decoration. When choosing between more movement and a clearer static matrix, choose the matrix. The desired finish is elegant and interactive, but the measure of success is whether a student can explain why the outcomes differ.

### DOMAIN AND IDENTITIES

Campus Rooms is a fictional room-booking service, not a real university system. Ari is U-01 and Bo is U-02. Use room C101 for the main examples and C202 when a second room is needed. B-001 is a representative booking owned by U-01. Campus staff may record room closures in the explicitly defined scenario, but do not have an unstated right to cancel every booking.

The recurring logical components are Booking, Policy, Room Catalog, Notification, and Storage. The default architecture is a modular application with an authoritative booking decision and separately tracked notification work. Logical modules do not automatically imply separate network services. No real personal data, credentials, or campus integration is used.

### CANONICAL BEHAVIORAL RULES

R-01 — No overlapping confirmed bookings for the same room. Intervals are half-open: [start,end). Thus 10:00–11:00 and 11:00–12:00 are adjacent, not overlapping. Different rooms do not conflict solely because their times match. R-02 — Booking duration is 30 through 120 minutes inclusive. Core examples use whole minutes, require end later than start, and do not impose a 30-minute slot grid. A temporary 90-minute upper bound must be labeled as a variant and reset afterward. R-03 — An authenticated requester may cancel their own future confirmed booking. “Future” means its start is later than the explicit current time. Already-started and other-owner cases are denied under this baseline. No general staff cancellation override is implied. R-04 — Notification failure does not undo a confirmed booking. Pending or failed notification work must remain visible and recoverable. Confirmation and notification are separate facts. R-05 — Users must be able to complete the core task with keyboard interaction and perceive meaningful status and error information. Examples demonstrate requirements and checks; they do not claim comprehensive accessibility certification. R-06 — Week 14 only: an effective closure blocks new confirmations overlapping its room and interval. Existing bookings remain recorded and are explicitly shown as affected, with an authorized resolution process and separately tracked communication. This is a labeled policy extension, not a retroactive rule in Weeks 1–13.

Room closure is a planted thread, not a leak of R-06 into earlier weeks. It appears as a stakeholder need in Week 1, as an open question and a scheduling condition in Week 2, and as a work card in Week 4, always unresolved; R-06 is its Week 14 resolution. Authoring agents keep it an open need until then.

### TIME, IDENTITY, AND STATE SEMANTICS

Use campus-local same-day time examples in the core. Do not introduce daylight-saving or cross-zone conversion silently. Any such extension needs its own explicit policy and fixtures. For cancellation checks use a fixed teaching clock, such as 09:00 for a booking starting at 10:00; the at-start case uses exactly 10:00.

The UI/request model may include Draft, Submitting, Confirmed, Rejected, and Unknown outcome. The baseline stored booking model contains Confirmed and Cancelled. A rejected request does not create a confirmed booking. A lost response can produce Unknown outcome in the UI even when the stored booking is Confirmed.

Scope operation keys to the authenticated requester and intended action. Repeating the same key with the same payload can return the original logical result; reusing it with a different payload produces an explicit conflict in the example. This is an application contract, not a claim that a timeout means no write or that every POST is inherently idempotent.

The no-overlap decision must survive concurrency. Correct local interval arithmetic is not enough. The animation must not imply that any transaction, an arbitrary row lock, or a unique room-plus-start key automatically protects every overlapping interval.

### QUALITY FIXTURES WITH DISTINCT BOUNDARIES

Q-01 is the illustrative search requirement introduced in Week 2: under the declared teaching fixture and 50 concurrent users, p95 search API response time is at most 500 ms at the service boundary. It is not a claim about a browser’s complete perceived experience or an industry benchmark. Actual performance evidence requires a specified dataset, request mix, environment, run conditions, and percentile calculation; the later twenty-point example illustrates the concept only.

Q-02 is the separate Week 11 illustrative objective: over seven days, at least 99% of eligible booking requests receive a correct outcome within two seconds. Eligible events are authenticated, syntactically valid booking requests reaching the stated service boundary, including legitimate room-conflict requests; health checks are excluded. A timely correct conflict rejection is good service. The fixed fixture contains 980 good events, 10 correct-but-slow results, 5 timeouts, and 5 unexpected server failures out of 1,000. The observed good-event rate is 98%, against an allowance of 10 bad events; 20 are observed. The allowance is the error budget. Observing twice the allowed bad-event rate is a burn rate of 2. Sustaining that rate under steady traffic from a fresh seven-day window would use its budget in 3.5 days. This is not remaining time after the displayed window, which has already exceeded its budget.

No energy or emissions value is supplied; the chart must not label the reduction as carbon savings.

### RETRY MODEL

The Week 11 retry-amplification scene uses a toy model: one dependency serves at most two attempts per round, first come first served; six unique requests Q1 to Q6 arrive in round 1; the first attempt of Q6 is dropped in round 1 without a response. A request still pending at the end of a round is a timeout for its client. Under the naive policy every timed-out client immediately submits another attempt while its earlier attempts stay queued, and an attempt served for a request that already succeeded is a duplicate effect. Under the bounded policy a client resubmits at most once, only after waiting two rounds and never while the circuit is open, and reports Unknown outcome once four rounds have passed with its retry spent; the circuit opens at the end of any round with at least four timeouts and blocks submissions for two round-ends.

Recorded results, recomputed by the checker: naive peaks at seven queued attempts, completes the six unique requests in round 5 with seven duplicate effects, and drains only in round 7; bounded peaks at three queued attempts, completes them in round 4 with no duplicate effects, and its circuit is open at the ends of rounds 1 and 2. The retries competed with the originals for the same capacity, which is the lesson. The model is a mechanism illustration, not a measurement, and it reuses the Week 6 operation-key idea for the duplicate effects.

### INVENTED DEPENDENCY

Week 13's change record uses textenc-turbo, a fictional package name marked unavailable in the course's registry fixture, to show a dependency invented by a code assistant. It is deliberately close to textenc, the fictional transitive dependency of Week 9; both names are course labels, and no assertion about their availability in public registries is made.

### AUTHORING NOTES FOR ALL FIXTURES

Any additional numbers introduced during authoring should be identified as measured, sourced, or simulated. Fix seeds where randomness is used for illustration, record the inputs, and independently verify the outputs. Repeated visits to a scene should not silently generate a different lesson.

Structured data for every Anchor scene, and for the numeric Bridges, lives in the anchors block of assets/demo-fixtures.json under the key wNN/scene-id, beside the cancellation matrix and the retry model. The HTML and the PDF render from it, and tools/check_plan.py recomputes every model and checks the week text against the values the fixtures say it shows.

# INSTRUCTOR PREFLIGHT AND ACCEPTANCE CHECKS

### BEFORE THE FIRST AUTHORED WEEK

Test the template at the intended recording resolution and at a 1280×720 viewing size representative of a smaller playback window. Establish shared component names, type sizes, diagram legends, and control labels. Decide the documented local-launch route and verify it on the intended teaching machine. # RECORDING TRACK: EVERY WEEK AS A NARRATED VIDEO

### SCOPE AND WHERE THE RULES LIVE

Every week is recorded (decision in 00_START_HERE.txt). The recordings follow the tutorial workspace rules, each of which lives in one file there and is not repeated here:

The lecture decks are the screen being recorded. A deck is not a slide show in the sense of those rules only when a hand operates it on camera: a picture that changes by itself is static under the action-over-static rule, and a pointer clicking Step, selecting a condition preset, hovering a node or dragging a slider is action.

### NO SPEAKING CUES IN THIS REPOSITORY

The repository and this plan hold no speaking cues (decision in 00_START_HERE.txt): no cue lists or shot lists, no narration text or narration briefs, no lines about what a moment of the deck teaches, no shot-duration budgets, and no recorded route that fixes which pages are shown or skipped, what is said over a page, or how a video opens and closes. Decks, fixtures, plan files, scene guides and completion records do not carry them, and authoring agents do not add them.

The recording agent generates all of it at run time. It first analyzes the week: plan/weekN.txt in full, plan_manifest.json, the case guide (19_recurring_case_and_demo_data.txt), the fixtures the deck renders, the scene guide, and the deck itself, operated in record=1 mode and rendered at 3840 by 2160. From that analysis it decides the route through the deck, writes the shot list and the narration, synthesizes the speech and records the tutorial, in the planning order and under the checks of the workspace rules above. What it writes for one recording stays with that recording in the tutorial workspace; it does not come back into this repository as cues for later runs.

### WHAT THE DECK PROVIDES FOR RECORDING

The shell implements the recording items of docs/scene-contract.md: the record=1 mode with deterministic timing and no autoplay, a pointer-target attribute (data-cue-target) on every control and every element a pointer may click, hover or drag, and the window.lecture interface, which lists the pages and reports each scene's state. The deck is laid out at 1920 by 1080 logical pixels and rendered in Chrome at device scale factor 2, which gives the native 3840 by 2160 capture the capture_scale rule requires; takes are captured at 60 fps. The dark theme is the default (00_START_HERE.txt).

The deck uses the whole canvas. Nothing is reserved for the presenter; the avatar overlay yields wherever the footage shows content in its rectangle, as the avatar block of the layout policy defines. Where a composition allows, the shell leaves the bottom-right corner empty so the overlay can appear more often. This is a preference, not a reservation.

Every reveal is operated. Anchors advance by Step clicks. Bridge rows, cells and arrows appear on a click or hover of their pointer target, never on a timer, so a Bridge counts as action when the pointer operates it. A running model (queue flow, retry amplification) counts as a process while its progress is visible on screen and is started by a visible Run click. In record=1 mode the deck never advances on its own and never draws a synthetic cursor; the real pointer moves with real input events.

The deck is deterministic, so no separate rehearsal is recorded or performed (00_START_HERE.txt). For its analysis the recording agent can render every page, condition and state at 3840 by 2160 with python scripts/inspect_deck.py --layout --week NN --shots --out <folder> and read the captures natively, and it serves the deck with python scripts/serve.py --root site.

### WORDING OF DECK TEXT AND NARRATION

We teach, so the narration is confident and sure (decision of 16 September 2026, clarified 17 September 2026: the rule is the stance, not a word list). The course's technical words are used as instruction with their ordinary meaning: verify, confirm, evidence, distinguish, establish, record. What stays out is the defensive shape: does not prove, no guarantee, another configuration may differ, we do not claim, record the evidence for a report. The viewer is addressed as "you"; never students, class, exam or grade; no production logistics in speech.

The deck text follows the same wording rules, and the style checker is run on the deck strings (python scripts/check_deck_text.py) as well as on the narration the recording agent generates.

### DELIVERABLES PER RECORDED WEEK

Generated at run time and kept with the recording in the tutorial workspace: the shot list passing the static-content checker, the 3840 by 2160 state captures, the narration units passing the style checker, the synthesized units, the takes recorded to them and remuxed, the edit plan, the placement passing the coverage checker, the Resolve project and timeline exports, the separate SRT and source mapping, the pre-delivery preflight report and the post-render check reports.
